🚑 Dental Office Emergency Drug Reference

Reference aid only. Activate EMS for any life-threatening emergency, give oxygen, and follow your office protocol. Doses are drawn from AAPD Medical Emergencies resource, AAP Drugs Used to Treat Pediatric Emergencies (2020), and AHA PALS/BLS/ACLS. Concentrations and devices vary by manufacturer: verify against the label, your DSO protocol, and your own training before use. Enter a weight to see calculated doses.

Oral midazolam (sedation, not an emergency drug)

Midazolam oral syrup 2 mg/mL

AAPD/AAP guideline range 0.25–0.5 mg/kg, max 20 mg. Up to 1 mg/kg may be used in ages 6 months–5 years. Lower doses when combined with other agents.
Requires appropriate sedation permit, pre-sedation evaluation, NPO guidance, continuous pulse oximetry and capnography, and a trained observer. Flumazenil should be available for reversal. Reduce dose for combined sedatives, opioids, or medically complex patients. Dispense from syrup with an oral syringe.
Sources: AAPD Reference Manual, Medical Emergencies resource; Shenoi & Timm, Pediatrics 2020;145(1):e20193450; AHA PALS/ACLS guidelines. Not a substitute for clinical judgment.
